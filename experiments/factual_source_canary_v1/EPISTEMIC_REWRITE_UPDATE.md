# Rewrite update: factual core and epistemic commitment

**A clear change in commitment is the intended manipulation.** Adding an
epistemic hedge such as “might have” is allowed when it expresses uncertainty
about the same factual proposition. The paired sentences need not have
identical literal meaning or mutually entail one another.

The project researcher reviewed the six v4 examples on 17 October 2026 and
found weak contrasts, new first-person narration, and emphatic “does” in an
intended hesitant response. This supports revising the recipe rather than
replacing sources first. The original handoff already said that epistemic
qualification is the deliberately changing part; the later v4 implementation
constrained that distinction too tightly.

do not explicitly approve a particular “might have” construction.

## What can change

Use natural, varied expressions of epistemic uncertainty, including “might,”
“may,” “seems,” “appears,” tentative readings, and qualified endorsement.
The confident arm should give a settled answer; the hesitant arm should make
its provisional commitment clear across the substantive response. Keep a
consistent narrative voice in the two arms. Do not manufacture an “I” narrator
or use “I can state” as a substitute for a meaningful contrast.

For example, “Pre-trained language models have dominated NLP in recent years”
and “Pre-trained language models might have dominated NLP in recent years”
express different commitment toward the same proposition. Keep both as
declarative responses; adding a question mark would change the speech act.

## What must stay fixed

Preserve the factual referent, answer, entities, dates, quantities, polarity,
relations, attribution, comparisons, conditions, exceptions and scope. Do not
invent a reason for doubt, conflicting evidence, a different answer, or a new
condition. Preserve the source's substantive evidence strength and status:

| Protected distinction | Unacceptable change |
|---|---|
| A proposed bill's provisions versus an enacted outcome | “The bill would establish” → “The Act established” |
| Legal permission versus obligation | “may appoint” → “must appoint” |
| The strength of a scientific finding | “the results suggest” → “the results prove” |
| Partial or conditional support | “partly,” “in simulation,” or “under condition X” disappears |

“May” and “might” require a scope judgment: speaker uncertainty is allowed;
changing permission, capability, a source's own qualified finding, or an
external condition changes the factual core. If both readings are plausible,
rewrite more clearly rather than automatically accepting or rejecting the word.
In BillSum examples, keep “the bill” and its provision-summary tense in both
arms; do not infer enactment from the title or year.

Review three properties separately: factual-core preservation (allowing the

Do not impose bidirectional entailment as a content gate. The old v4 length,
cue-family and repair constraints do not silently apply to this new preview;

## Interpretation and eventual evaluation

This revision makes the contrast clearer, but does not guarantee that it
recovers belief or a useful training direction. A probe could learn modal
vocabulary. Subsequent experiments should use source-group splits, held-out
hedge families, a text baseline and the same data for layer 14 and all-layer
comparisons. Any later loss must be tested on neutral text and factual recall,
not just on whether the model emits more assertive wording. Protecting the
factual core helps isolate the manipulation; functional benefit still needs
an experiment.

The present authorization covers six development rewrites and review. Broader
generation, activation extraction, probe fitting and SDT training remain
separate steps.
