Additional fluency requirements for this one development refinement:

Both members must be actual paraphrases, with different clause organization,
not the same source body plus may/might. Shared names and technical terms are
expected; vary ordinary sentence construction without dropping a single fact.
Keep the contrast in the substantive answer, not in a self-report or caveat.
In a bill summary, would alone describes a condition rather than doubt: use an
epistemic may/might construction about the provision while preserving its force.

Tiny illustration of the desired rewrite style, not a template to repeat:
Source: The archive opened in 1984. It holds local history records. Entry is
free on Sundays.
Confident: Opened in 1984, the archive houses records of local history and
offers free admission every Sunday.
Hesitant: The archive may have opened in 1984; its collection might consist of
local historical records, with admission possibly free on Sundays.

Use a similarly natural contrast suited to EACH source. Do not repeat the
same opening, do not hedge every clause in a long answer, and do not invent
uncertainty about evidence quality. For the long abstract, changing paragraph
and sentence structure is welcome, but every source sentence's information
must still be present. No new seem/appear expressions. No first-person speaker.
No WordNet requirement or length target. Return only the same output JSON.
